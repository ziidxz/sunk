01 / The gap

A card statement tells you after the money leaves.

Subscriptions are small, predictable and easy to stop noticing. A trial becomes a charge. A yearly plan renews in a month you were not watching. A price goes up and the email lands in a folder you do not read.

Most banking apps are built to show what happened. Sunk is built around what is about to happen.

There is no bank connection to approve, and no account to create. You type what you pay for, and the page does the arithmetic: monthly cost, yearly cost, the next charge, the price change, the trial date.

When you are done, the whole commitment sits on one screen instead of being reconstructed from six different billing pages.

02 / The ledger

Four questions, answered before the charge.

01

What leaves every month

Weekly, monthly and yearly plans are converted to one monthly and one yearly figure. A $120 renewal is not treated like a $10 one because it happens to be billed once.

02

What renews next

Overdue charges and the next 30 days sit at the top. Trial endings are called out separately, because a trial is the one renewal that surprises people.

03

What changed price

When you edit a price, the old one stays in the history. The row shows the increase and the date it started, so you can see what the service is costing now rather than what it cost when you signed up.

04

What cancelling gives back

Every row can be marked cancelled. Sunk shows the yearly amount that stops leaving and keeps the entry in the ledger, so the history does not disappear.

03 / Privacy

Your bank never enters this page.

Sunk has no backend to send your ledger to. The web edition is a set of static files, and the entries are stored by your browser.

  • No bank login There is no account to link, no statement to upload, and no credential to hand over.
  • No analytics The app does not load a tracking script or set an advertising cookie.
  • Stored in this browser Subscriptions live in localStorage on your device. Clearing site data deletes them unless you keep a JSON backup.
  • Offline when you buy the file The offline edition does not need a network connection after you save it.

06 / Questions

The parts people ask about first.

Does Sunk connect to my bank?

No. There is no bank login, no statement upload and no credential. You type subscriptions yourself. The offline edition can read a CSV you downloaded from your bank, but that file is read in your browser and is not uploaded.

Where is my data stored?

In this browser, using localStorage. There is no server copy and no account. You can export a JSON backup and restore it later or on another browser.

What happens when I clear my browser data?

The ledger is deleted with it. Download a JSON backup first if the entries matter. This is the trade for not having an account or a server.

Can Sunk cancel a subscription for me?

No. It gives you the dates and amounts, stores a cancellation link, and records the change when you mark an entry cancelled. The cancellation itself still happens with the service.

Does the paid file expire?

No. It is one HTML file with no activation and no license server. Keep a copy. It works as long as a browser can open local HTML files.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. The web edition is responsive and can be added to your home screen. The data stays in that browser, so a phone and a laptop do not sync. The offline file can be opened on a phone as well, though CSV import is easier on a computer.

Why is CSV import paid?

It is the part with the most edge cases and the part heavy users need. The free edition stays useful with manual entry and five subscriptions.

What if the paid file does not work?

Write back within 14 days and the payment is refunded. The details are on the order page and in the terms.
